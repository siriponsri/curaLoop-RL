## 4. Research direction now accepted

Main question:

> Under matched public information, ordinary tools, model and resource limits,
> does access to a prepared executable implementation of requirements improve
> native scientific-task completion over complete written check definitions?

The paper direction is empirical scientific-software reliability, with the
pharmaceutical workbench as an application example. It does not claim novel
RL screening, a new benchmark, molecular discovery benefit, or business value.

Three intended contributions require evidence:

1. An external, paired estimate of prepared-check benefit or lack of benefit.
2. Reproducible analysis of local-check/native-success disagreements and
   actual checker/repair opportunities.
3. Preparation and runtime cost, including failures and unavailable diagnostics.

[Rules to Tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00313) already studies prepared
executable public scientific checks. A different dataset alone is insufficient
to establish a strong additional contribution. LR-SAR1 needs interpretable
external findings, a fair intervention and transparent cost/failure evidence.
