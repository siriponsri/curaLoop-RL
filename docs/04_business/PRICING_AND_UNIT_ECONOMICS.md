# Pricing and unit economics

**All figures below are illustrative assumptions in USD. They are not observed costs, market prices, a quotation or revenue projections.** The first price is unset until discovery establishes scope and a delivery estimate.

## Pricing logic

A pilot price must cover delivery labor, support/rework, compute, purchased components if any and commercial overhead. Local execution can reduce hosting needs but does not eliminate analyst or support labor. Count the owner's time even if no salary is paid. A fixed-price engagement becomes unprofitable if data cleaning or integration scope expands without a change mechanism.

| Scenario | Assumed fee | Delivery hours | Labor rate | Infrastructure | Other/support allowance | Contribution |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Lean | 1,500 | 20 | 35 | 25 | 100 | 675 (45.0%) |
| Base | 3,000 | 40 | 50 | 75 | 250 | 675 (22.5%) |
| Stress | 3,000 | 70 | 60 | 150 | 350 | -1,700 (-56.7%) |

Contribution equals fee minus delivery hours times labor rate minus infrastructure minus other/support allowance. It excludes tax, acquisition cost, general overhead and unpriced liability; it is not net profit. The companion CSV is a reproducible calculation of these assumptions. Negative scenarios are retained because they reveal the risk of a services-first model.

## Quote construction

Separate a public-data evaluation, customer data adapter, additional endpoint mapping, on-site support, hosting and maintenance. Specify the number of datasets, policies and report iterations. Record assumptions about input quality and supported operating systems. Include a pause/change-order path when data is unusable or access is unavailable. Do not promise a scientific outcome to justify the fee.

## Subscription decision

Recurring pricing requires a recurring job: repeated campaign comparisons, managed upgrades, support or an integration maintained over time. Define support hours, supported versions, response expectations and data handling before selling a subscription. A local MIT application can be copied; differentiation must come from actual service and workflow value rather than artificial claims of code exclusivity.

## Buyer value measurement

For a customer, compare measured analyst hours and successful reproducibility against the prior workflow. Model an assay-spending scenario only with measured or explicitly assumed assay costs, and report uncertainty. The value of a historical active does not equal the value of a viable drug. Avoid multiplying simulated hit gains by a speculative drug revenue figure.

## Market sizing

No TAM/SAM/SOM estimate is supplied because no reliable account registry, buying-rate estimate or validated price exists. Start with a bottom-up list of eligible account types, their procurement paths and feasible annual delivery capacity. Add market totals only after sourcing and documenting each input. The business model must remain useful even if only a small number of pilots can be delivered.

## Integration and objective risk

The illustrative fee scenarios are unchanged; no new price validation was performed. Add a delivery estimate for label reconciliation, backend version compatibility and report reproducibility before preparing an actual quote. An adapter may reduce development work, but that saving is a hypothesis until effort is measured. The buyer is not purchasing a guaranteed hit count or another paper's percentage reduction in experiments.
