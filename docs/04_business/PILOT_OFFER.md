# R&D screening-planning pilot offer

**Draft offer for owner review. Not sent to a customer.**

## Intended buyer and objective

The pilot is for a research team with a defined molecular screening decision and a historical assay table that it is permitted to use. Its objective is to determine whether a local, auditable replay workbench improves comparison, explanation or reproducibility of candidate-selection decisions. Demonstrating improved biological outcomes requires a separate evidence design.

## Deliverables

1. A local installation in an agreed environment, with reproducible launch and backup instructions.
2. A data-readiness report: endpoint mapping, structure validity, missingness, duplicates, exclusions and allowed use.
3. A jointly fixed replay protocol: candidate pool, initial knowledge, policies, operational assumptions and endpoints.
4. A matched comparison package with public/private fields separated and complete run provenance.
5. A decision report describing performance, uncertainty, failure cases and sensitivity to assumptions.
6. An operator walkthrough and a handover containing the exact tested release, environment and recovery procedure.

Use public data for the first demonstration. Customer data ingestion, adapters and asynchronous extensions are planned capabilities requiring scoped implementation. The offer must list their actual delivery state at contract time. Do not sell a specification as an installed feature.

## Inputs needed from the buyer

An accountable project contact; a stated use case; permitted structures/features and outcomes; assay label semantics; any usable cost/time records; a local execution environment; and acceptance criteria. If operational timestamps are unavailable, present multiple declared scenarios. Do not estimate real-world savings by treating assumed costs as measured costs.

## Acceptance

The buyer can launch the agreed release, reproduce one campaign, inspect every accepted request and recover an exported run. The comparison uses the agreed data and protocol. Counts reconcile to the event ledger. The report distinguishes historical activity labels from predictions, missing outcomes and pending work. The handover records limitations and unresolved integration issues. Scientific superiority is not an acceptance requirement unless explicitly contracted as a research hypothesis with a meaningful failure outcome.

## Scope boundaries

The pilot does not execute wet-lab experiments, promise drug efficacy, authorize clinical or regulated decisions, provide enterprise multi-tenant hosting or guarantee a particular hit rate. Hosting, security hardening, live laboratory integrations, proprietary model licensing and ongoing support are separately scoped. Any intended use expansion needs a concrete review of that customer's workflow.

## Commercial structure

Use a fixed scope and milestone-based acceptance: data readiness, working replay and final handover. Quote a price only after discovery and a delivery estimate. The illustrative unit-economics scenarios are internal planning aids, not a customer quote. Record rework limits, support period, permitted disclosures and exit/export conditions in an agreed statement of work.

## Evidence capture

Measure installation effort, analyst hands-on time, reproducibility success, questions answered and whether a research decision changed. Record willingness to continue, actual payment and repeated use independently. A polite positive reaction to a demo is not a purchase commitment. Keep all customer-specific evidence private unless publication permission is explicit.

## Comparison and measurement plan

Before quoting implementation, compare the required workflow with the buyer's existing notebook, internal process or established BO framework. Agree one replay task and a small set of questions. Record current analyst hands-on effort, reproduction success and output usability, then repeat the agreed exercise with the tested deliverable. Report comparable measurements and limitations; an uncontrolled improvement impression is not causal evidence of laboratory savings.

The pilot entry gate requires resolvable assay identity, permitted use and a feasible local environment. Resolve endpoint/label conflicts during data readiness before comparing methods. A public-data demonstration may proceed while customer ingestion remains unimplemented. If the client already uses BayBE or BoTorch, evaluate an adapter with a named version and fixed acceptance contract. Do not borrow BayBE's example savings percentages for the offer.
