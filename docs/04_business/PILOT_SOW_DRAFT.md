# Pilot statement of work: negotiation draft

**Unexecuted draft. Buyer, fee, dates, support commitments and authorized signatories are unassigned. No agreement is created by this file.** Confirm customer-specific terms through the appropriate contracting process before work begins.

## Purpose and deliverables

Evaluate whether a local molecular-screening replay workflow supports an identified R&D planning decision. Deliver installation, data-readiness assessment, an agreed experiment protocol, matched replay results, a limitations report and an operator handover. The exact supported release and dataset manifest form part of the agreed scope.

## Customer responsibilities

The customer identifies an accountable scientist and data owner, provides permitted inputs with endpoint definitions, supplies a supported environment and reviews milestone outputs. The customer identifies any intended regulated, clinical or safety-critical use before scope agreement. Lack of data access pauses dependent work rather than authorizing a substitute dataset without agreement.

## Supplier responsibilities

The supplier preserves input versions, documents transformations, separates hidden labels from decisions and reports failures accurately. The supplier identifies components that are existing capabilities versus scoped development. Deliveries include machine-readable exports and installation/recovery instructions. No prospective outcome, assay hit rate or laboratory saving is guaranteed.

## Milestones and acceptance

Data readiness is accepted when the agreed table can be mapped or its limitations are documented. Replay capability is accepted when the agreed campaign executes and counts reconcile. Final handover is accepted when the customer can reproduce a comparison and export/recover a run. Define the review interval, correction process, maximum iterations and payment milestones in the signed agreement.

## Data and intellectual property schedule

List input ownership and permitted processing, processing location, authorized access, retention/deletion, backup, incident contact and permitted publication. Distinguish existing MIT code, third-party software, licensed datasets, newly commissioned adapters and customer-confidential outputs. Any special ownership or licensing allocation must be written explicitly; the draft does not alter already granted open-source rights.

## Change control and exit

New datasets, endpoints, integrations, hosting or security requirements require an impact estimate and scope decision. On exit, supply the agreed exports and remove access according to the agreed data schedule. State what maintenance ends and which customer-owned artifacts remain usable. Keep an evidence copy only where the agreement permits it.

## Unresolved commercial fields

Before signature determine parties and authority, scope version, fee/currency/tax handling, expenses, acceptance intervals, support period, data/IP terms, confidentiality, responsibility limits, dispute process and termination. These fields require a customer-specific agreement; no jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion is provided here.

## Technical appendix to agree before delivery

Identify the tested release, source data/assay mapping, initialization regime, comparator versions, metric definitions and assumed versus measured timing/cost fields. Mark unresolved label/version questions as data-readiness findings. Identify any planned adapter explicitly; acceptance follows a demonstrated installation and reproducible replay, not completion of this specification. Scientific negative or inconclusive results remain valid research deliverables when the agreed protocol was executed and reported faithfully.
