# Business strategy: evidence before scale

**Status: BUSINESS_HYPOTHESES.** The owner selected an R&D-team pilot as the first commercial direction. No customer, purchase order, validated saving or revenue is recorded. This strategy defines experiments that can establish or reject a business opportunity.

## Initial problem hypothesis

A small screening team may have historical assay data but lack a convenient way to compare next-batch choices under its own budget and turnaround constraints. A planning report can be useful before a team trusts automated recommendations. The proposed initial service combines local installation, data mapping, matched replay, an interpretable evidence report and training. Interview actual users to establish how often this problem occurs and what currently solves it.

## Roles and entry segment

Start with one segment: a pharmaceutical/biotech discovery team or contract research group that already has a structured historical compound/assay table. The scientist uses the tool; the informatics lead assesses integration; a research manager or budget owner approves a pilot; data/security staff decide what may leave the environment. These role assignments are hypotheses, not a discovered account map.

The initial product should work with public data so buyer evaluation can start without disclosure of a proprietary library. A customer-data replay is a separate phase after scope, rights and access are agreed. The current local-only application supports a demonstration, not unattended multi-user enterprise production.

## Value proposition to test

“Reproduce and compare molecular screening decisions using your declared constraints, with a traceable record of what was known at each decision.” The immediate outcome is a decision-quality and workflow assessment. An increase in experimentally validated hits or a reduction in actual spending is a later, independently measured outcome.

Sell the implementation and analysis service, not an exclusive claim to MIT-licensed code. Potential revenue comes from pilot delivery, data adapters, support, training and maintenance. A subscription is justified only if repeated use and support needs exist. A paper can establish credibility and explain limits; it does not demonstrate willingness to pay.

## Position and differentiation

The intended advantage is a constrained, reproducible evaluation workflow usable by a small team on local infrastructure. Existing tools already offer powerful acquisition, experiment planning and active-learning orchestration. Assess whether packaging CuraLoop around a specific customer workflow is useful enough to buy, or whether contributing an adapter to an established tool would be a better product decision.

## Milestones and stop conditions

Advance from public demo to a customer-data pilot only when a buyer identifies a meaningful decision, a data owner can authorize the input and the deliverables have explicit acceptance criteria. Advance from service pilot to repeat product only after repeated use or a real support commitment. If customers already solve the problem satisfactorily with existing open tools, narrow the offer to a specialized integration or stop that segment. Do not respond to weak demand by adding unrelated AI features.

## Separate public and private records

Publish nonconfidential scientific results and generic offer materials. Keep account names, quotes, interview notes, proprietary structures, commercial negotiations and credentials outside public Git. Use anonymous case studies only with permission and enough detail to avoid implying an identifiable endorsement. No outreach is authorized by this package.

## Updated competitive thesis

The full BayBE article makes generic backtesting and asynchronous optimization weak differentiators. The initial commercial hypothesis is a bounded research service: translate an assay table into a defensible replay, compare relevant choices, explain operational assumptions and hand over reproducible evidence. A thin adapter can be the deliverable when it solves the buyer's gap more efficiently than additional independent infrastructure. No integration is implemented by this documentation package.

Validate three separate questions in discovery: does the team have a recurring decision problem; does the proposed report change or clarify that decision; and will the buyer commit resources or payment to a scoped delivery? Keep these observations separate from a scientific superiority claim. Neither publication plans nor third-party headline gains establish demand.
