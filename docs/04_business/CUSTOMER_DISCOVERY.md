# Customer-discovery plan

**Status: INTERVIEWS_NOT_RUN.** These questions and decision thresholds are proposals for owner-authorized discovery. No messages, interviews or commercial commitments were sent or made during package creation.

## Hypotheses

H-B1: teams repeatedly choose assay batches under constraints that their current tools do not represent clearly. H-B2: reproducing a strategy comparison is costly enough to justify help. H-B3: local execution and explicit provenance reduce an actual adoption obstacle. H-B4: a budget owner can fund a bounded evaluation. H-B5: a pilot can be delivered with acceptable solo support effort.

## Interview guide

Ask for the last real campaign rather than a general opinion about AI. What decision was made? Which data existed at that moment? Who selected the next batch? What had to wait, and why? Which software or spreadsheet was used? What would a different decision have changed? Which data or timestamps can be shared? Who would approve an evaluation, what would success look like and what would stop purchase?

Show the public-data demo only after understanding the current workflow. Ask the participant to identify which parts answer a real question and which duplicate current tools. Do not lead with “Would this save money?” or suggest a desired benefit. If allowed, ask them to reproduce a task and describe difficulties. Record statements and observed actions separately from the owner's interpretation.

## Evidence record

Use an interview code, date, role, segment, permission status, described workflow, existing alternative, concrete pain, data availability, buying process, budget evidence, next commitment and disconfirming evidence. Do not put identifying notes in the public repository. A public synthesis can report aggregated themes only with an appropriate permission basis.

## Proposed discovery milestone

Begin with five relevant conversations, seeking repeated evidence of the same workflow problem, at least two feasible data situations and one concrete pilot next step. These are managerial decision thresholds, not statistically representative market validation. If interviews contradict the assumed problem, revise the segment or offer before adding features.

## Stronger and weaker signals

An agreed evaluation dataset, named buyer, booked internal review or signed paid pilot is stronger than enthusiasm. Actual repeated use is stronger than a one-time trial. Record nonresponses, objections, inability to share data and satisfactory existing alternatives. Do not convert a memorandum of interest into revenue or an unpaid pilot into willingness to pay.

## Follow-up decisions

For each hypothesis record supported, contradicted or unresolved with the underlying evidence. Choose whether to proceed, narrow, integrate with an existing tool or stop the segment. Seek separate authorization before contacting anyone, sending the draft offer or publishing a customer case study.

## Questions sharpened by the full-text software review

Ask which tool already handles experiment planning, whether historical backtesting is used, and which exact step still takes analyst time. Establish whether the pain is data semantics, installation, pending-result tracking, comparative analysis or report handover. Ask for an example decision and the current evidence needed to approve it. Record an alternative that solved the problem as a disconfirmation, not as a lead to pressure.

For each pilot hypothesis record the current workflow, measured baseline effort, proposed change, acceptance condition and observation method. Paper-level efficiency claims are context only. They cannot substitute for measured customer effort, agreed purchasing intent or actual payment. No outreach is authorized or performed by this document.
