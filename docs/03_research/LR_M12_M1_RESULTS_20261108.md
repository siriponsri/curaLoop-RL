## What ran

The six previously exposed FS-Mol development compound pools were authenticated
against their prior hashes. Before decoding their binary labels, the admission
checked the frozen public feature identities and existing protection index for
canonical-compound, assay and annotation-group conflicts. All six pools passed:
1,285 task rows in four broad annotation groups. These are not newly independent
families. Only those selected pools were copied; an exposed assay name did not
authorize its complete raw source.

The [frozen diagnostic design](../../research/configs/lr_m12_m1_design_20261108_v1.json)
selected the largest and smallest admitted pools by row count: CHEMBL1738549 and
CHEMBL2219098. Nested pools of 32/64/128 compounds were selected by an ID hash;
initial labels were uniformly selected with fixed seed 101, counts 0 or 16.
There were **12 adapter cases and 36 one-decision timing calls**, not 36 molecular
discovery campaigns. Tasks, sizes and settings were frozen before these fits.

The adapter uses the preserved Tanimoto logistic Bernoulli GP Laplace code,
three fixed kernel scales and 128 posterior function particles per context.
Hypothetical future observations reweight the fixed particles. This is a proxy
for future GP refitting, not an exact account of the retrained campaign policy.
All variants share public fingerprints, delivered labels, prediction particles,
candidate menu, Greedy continuation and cache opportunities. Rollout horizon is
four, shortlist eight, maximum 4,096 Greedy states / 1,024 paired traces / 0.75
seconds per call. Horizon four does not optimize the full remaining budget.

The policy/predictor interfaces reject outcome fields and oracle keyword inputs;
17 constructed checks passed, including deterministic prediction, one-class
starts, invalid forecasts, last-step Greedy and interrupted incomplete scores.
These are interface/mechanism tests. They do not establish process-level security,
real-world safety or independent scientific validation. The evaluator holds the
admitted labels; only declared initial observations enter these diagnostic fits.

Revision 2 exhausted its **paired-trace cap**, rather than its wall-time cap, in

`r(n) = 4 sqrt(2 log(2 * 3 * 7 * 4 / 0.05) / n)`.

Even at 64 samples per model/action, the radius is about 2.02 hits over a
four-step forecast. Completing that stage for all alternatives requires 1,344

the frozen forecast only. No positive lower bound or actual discovery benefit
was established. More samples might tighten this bound, but would not resolve
the separate novelty failure; no post-result cap increase was made.

Positive point estimates and different choices are not realized assay gains.

## Formal reduction and literature gate

Let `G` be common-mixture Greedy and `G[a]` force action `a` once then follow
that same adaptive Greedy. Revision 1 selects a positive estimated
`min_m E_m[U(G[a]) - U(G)]`, otherwise `G`. Restricting the policy class to
`{G} union {G[a]}` and the uncertainty set to the finite declared models maps
this objective directly to robust baseline regret. This is our algebraic
reduction; it does not transfer the source paper's MDP guarantees. The within-model
baseline subtraction and zero-valued baseline are already explicit in equation
2 and Section 3 of [Petrik et al. (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.03842).

Revision 2 adds fixed-stage Hoeffding bounds, elimination and retention of a
completed baseline-relative alternative. Adaptive rollout allocation and
confidence-based stopping have established precedents: inspected Sections 4/5
and the Hoeffding discussion of [Dimitrakakis and Lagoudakis (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2015).
That paper allocates across states; this implementation screens action/model
comparisons. The distinction does not by itself establish a substantive advance.
We did not reproduce their algorithm or claim exact implementation equivalence.

[ENS (Jiang et al., 2017)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jiang17d.html)
already addresses cumulative binary active search with a remaining-budget
approximation; selected objective/method passages were inspected. Our four-step
rollout is not ENS and cannot inherit its results. [MCTS-SPIBB (Castellini et al.,
2023)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/castellini23a.html) already combines
online search and baseline-constrained improvement. We inspected its Section 4
algorithm and Section 5 theorem statements, not the complete appendix or code.
Our finite forecast has no trajectory-count SPIBB safety guarantee.

The candidate's matched robust control is Revision 1 itself. Calling it a new
arm while using an uncached or otherwise deprived robust comparator would not
identify a new mechanism. Generic cache reuse, finite-model adaptation and
bounded interruption do not supply the required distinction. Both declared
revisions are retained; no favorable molecular outcome was used to invent a
third family. The [M0 review](../02_literature/METHOD_FIRST_CLOSEST_WORK_20261108.md)
remains historical evidence, with this narrower executed follow-up.
